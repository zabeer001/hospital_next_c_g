## They begin with shared context

Connected teams do not depend on one person carrying the full picture. They create a shared, dependable source of context that lets each role understand what has happened and what needs attention next.

## They make ownership visible

Clear ownership prevents quiet gaps. When a patient, follow-up, or operational task has an obvious owner, people spend less time asking who is responsible and more time moving the work forward.

- Assign responsibility where work begins.
- Keep handoffs specific and visible.
- Review unresolved items at a predictable time.

## They learn from everyday patterns

Teams improve when they can see their own rhythm. Simple trends—patient volume, follow-up completion, workload by specialty—can reveal where a process is working and where support is needed.

The goal is not surveillance. It is shared awareness that makes planning easier and helps the team respond before small problems become larger ones.
