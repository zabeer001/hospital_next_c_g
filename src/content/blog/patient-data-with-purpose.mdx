## Start with the decision

A dashboard is only useful when it supports a decision. Before adding a number, ask what someone should understand or do differently after seeing it.

Patient volume may shape staffing. Follow-up completion may point to a process issue. Distribution across doctors may reveal an uneven workload. Each measure should have a clear operational purpose.

## Prefer signals over decoration

More visualizations do not create more insight. A small collection of stable, well-defined measures is easier to understand and more likely to become part of the team's routine.

- Define each metric in plain language.
- Show a useful comparison or time period.
- Keep underlying records easy to reach.
- Remove charts that do not change a decision.

## Keep people in the picture

Aggregated data can guide planning, but it should never turn patients into anonymous throughput. Good systems make it easy to move from a pattern back to the people and care relationships behind it.

Useful data creates perspective without losing empathy.
